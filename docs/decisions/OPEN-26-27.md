# `OPEN-26`, `OPEN-27` — Freshness on update, and what counts as a change

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision IDs** | `DEC-19` (`OPEN-26`), `DEC-20` (`OPEN-27`) |
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-09-13 |
| **Owner** | A |
| **Blocks released** | Phase 6 |
| **Affects** | `REQ-TOOL-013`, `REQ-VER-001..009`, `REQ-DATA-003`, implementation plan §7.1, `N-07` |

Two questions that decide whether Update Research is worth pressing. The first
decides whether an update actually looks again; the second decides whether what
it reports back is news or noise.

---

## `DEC-19` — Cache lifetime and freshness (`OPEN-26`)

**The retrieval cache lives for one run and never crosses a run, a session or
a user.** Within a run, an entry is reused for `RETRIEVAL_CACHE_TTL_SECONDS`
(default 900, the run's wall-clock ceiling). Update Research therefore
re-fetches every category it asks, by construction.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Cache lifetime | One run. Configurable within it (`REQ-TOOL-013 AC-4`). |
| Which retrievals bypass the cache on update? | **All of them.** An update starts a new run, and a new run starts with an empty cache. There is no bypass flag to forget to set. |
| Is evidence reused across versions? | **No.** Every version holds its own sources and evidence with its own retrieval timestamps (`REQ-VER-003 AC-2`). This closes `N-07`. |
| What does the update prioritise? | Areas whose evidence is most likely to have moved, first (`REQ-VER-003 AC-3`). See below. |

### Why not a longer-lived cache

A shared cache would save money on two people asking about the same company
within an hour. Rejected for three reasons, each sufficient:

1. **Privacy.** A cache keyed on queries is a record of what users researched,
   readable by whichever run hits it next. The documents category makes this
   worse: a document search result is somebody's file.
2. **`REQ-VER-003` would become a discipline.** An update would have to
   remember to bypass it, per category, forever. A run-scoped cache makes
   freshness structural, the same way version-scoping made immutability
   structural (implementation plan §4.1).
3. **The cost it saves is bounded elsewhere.** `TBD-10` and `TBD-11` cap a run's
   spend whatever the cache does, and Phase 8 sets them.

### Prioritisation: what is most likely to have changed

An update re-asks the questions the previous version planned, rather than
planning afresh, so the two versions are measured against the same questions
and comparison is like with like. It orders the areas before researching them,
so a budget that runs out cuts the least volatile area rather than the most:

| Priority | An area is here when |
|---|---|
| **1 — stale** | Evidence it relied on is past its staleness window for its metric class (`DEC-10 §5`: share price after 1 day, counts after 90, currency and ratio figures after 400). |
| **2 — volatile** | It is researched through news, financial data or job postings, which change between any two dates. |
| **3 — stable** | Filings, web pages, and anything else. |

Ties keep the previous plan's order. The ordering and the reason for each area
are recorded in the step checkpoint and shown in the activity feed as "Checking
what may have changed".

## `DEC-20` — What counts as a meaningful difference (`OPEN-27`)

**A difference is meaningful when the evidence changed, not when the words
did.** Two runs of the same model over the same facts will phrase them
differently every time, so wording alone is never reported as change.

### The comparison

Every claim in the new version is matched to at most one claim in the version
it updates. Two claims match when their wording, **with every figure and date
removed**, shares at least 60% of its significant words. Figures are removed so
that "revenue was $1.2bn" and "revenue was $1.4bn" match, and the difference
between them is found by comparing evidence rather than text.

For each match, and each claim left unmatched, exactly one of these applies:

| Kind | Meaningful when | Reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Figure changed** | Both claims cite a comparable value for the same metric class and period, and they differ by more than the `DEC-10` tolerance for that class. | Before and after values, the period, and the source of the new figure. |
| **Newer period** | Both cite the same metric class, and the new figure's period ends later. | The old figure superseded by the newer period's. |
| **Conclusion changed** | An `analysis` or `forecast` claim's confidence level changed, its claim type changed, or its **assessment** changed: the words that carry direction or judgement ("strong", "weak", "rising", "falling", "ahead", "behind", and their kin in a fixed list) differ between the two. | Before and after, with the confidence change stated (`REQ-VER-007 AC-3`). |
| **Assumptions changed** | A forecast's stated assumptions share less than 60% of their significant words with the previous forecast's. | Both sets of assumptions. |
| **New finding** | A `fact`, `analysis` or `forecast` claim has no match, and cites at least one source the previous version did not. | The claim and the new source behind it. |
| **No longer found** | A previous `fact`, `analysis` or `forecast` claim has no match, **and none of the sources it cited were retrieved again**. | The old claim, worded as "not found again in this update", never as "no longer true". |
| **Gap closed** | A previous `uncertainty` matches a claim that is now evidenced. | What is now known. |
| **Gap opened** | A new `uncertainty` matches a previously evidenced claim. | What could not be confirmed this time. |

**Not meaningful, and never reported as change:**

- rewording of a matched claim whose evidence, type and confidence are the same;
- a figure within tolerance (`$1.2bn` against `$1,198m`);
- sources appearing or disappearing without changing any claim;
- a claim the new report did not write, or wrote unmatched, while the sources
  behind it are still there — that is the report choosing differently from the
  same evidence, which is wording at a larger scale;
- a confidence change on a `fact` claim with the same figure — that is
  corroboration moving, and it is shown in the claim, not announced.

### The masterplan's kinds of change

`REQ-VER-006 AC-2` names six. Each change is labelled with one, from what the
evidence is rather than from what the prose says:

| Label | Assigned when |
|---|---|
| Stock information | The metric class is share price. |
| Financial figures | The metric class is a currency or ratio figure, or the source is financial data or a filing. |
| Job postings | The source is job postings, or the claim counts roles, openings or hiring. |
| Forecast assumptions | The claim is a forecast. |
| New products | The claim names a launch, release, product or unveiling. |
| New competitors | The claim names a competitor, rival or new entrant. |
| Other developments | None of the above. |

### When nothing changed

The summary says so, in those words, and names the version and date it was
compared with (`REQ-VER-006 AC-3`). It never pads the list with rewording to
look busy.

### Explaining a changed conclusion

Each change carries the claim ids on both sides and the evidence ids of the new
claim (`REQ-VER-007 AC-1`), so the reader can open the new evidence in the new
version and the old conclusion in the old one (`AC-2`). The explanation text is
assembled from those rows. **No model writes it**: a model asked "what changed"
will find something to say, which is the exact failure `OPEN-27` exists to
prevent.

## What counts as a path to a new version

`REQ-VER-001 AC-2` says the Update Research control is the only path to a new
version. Phase 3 already opens one more: a follow-up question the evidence
cannot answer starts research of its own (`REQ-CONV-003`), and because a closed
version is immutable (`REQ-VER-002`), that research becomes a new version.

**Both paths are an explicit user action, and that is what `REQ-VER-001` exists
to protect** (`AC-1`: "no background process re-runs research on its own").
Neither is scheduled, automatic or triggered by anything but the reader. The two
are treated identically from here: both versions get a What's Changed summary,
and the version list labels each as "Update" or "Follow-up research" so the
reader knows why it exists.

This is recorded as an interpretation, not a silent one: if `AC-2` is meant
literally, follow-up research would instead have to write into an open version,
which `REQ-VER-002` forbids.

## Consequences

- **Schema:** none. `research_versions.change_summary` (jsonb) and
  `previous_version_id` are in the baseline; `research_runs.kind` already has
  `update`.
- **Pipeline:** an update run is `prioritize → research → synthesize → compare`;
  a follow-up run gains `compare` at the end. `interpret` and `plan` do not run
  on update, because the subject and the questions are what is being re-checked.
- **API:** `POST /v1/research/{id}/update`; the version payload carries its
  change summary and the version list its origin.
- **Testing:** comparison is a pure function over rows and is tested as one,
  with a fixture for every kind above and one for rewording that must report
  nothing.

---

## Known risk

**The 60% match threshold is a heuristic over wording, and it will occasionally
pair two claims that are about different things, or miss a pair that is the
same.** A wrong pairing reports a "figure changed" between two unrelated
figures — which the metric class and period guards make rare, because both must
also agree. A missed pairing reports a new finding and a claim no longer found,
which is noisy but not false. The threshold is one constant, and the trigger to
revisit it is a reader reporting a change that is not one.
